ME 588 Autonomous Mars Rover

A team-built rover for autonomous navigation and object manipulation. I led electronics, sensor integration and programming; 95% success on foam-cube detection, pickup and placement in arena trials.

The task

An arena, a set of foam cubes, and a rover that had to find them, pick them up and place them — with no operator in the loop. Fully autonomous operation was the requirement, not a stretch goal, which meant every failure mode had to be handled on the rover rather than recovered by a human.

What I owned

  • Electronics. Power distribution, motor drive and the sensor wiring that everything else depended on.
  • Sensor integration. Getting detection reliable enough that the autonomy above it could assume its inputs — which is where most of the debugging time actually went.
  • Autonomy software. The detection, approach, pickup and placement sequence, including the recovery behaviors that turn a 70% success rate into a 95% one.

The mechanical design and control system integration were collaborative — the arm geometry and the software that drove it were developed against each other rather than in sequence.

Why it hit 95%

The gap between a rover that works in a demo and one that works in trials is almost entirely in the failure handling. A cube detected at a bad angle, a grasp that slips, an approach that overshoots — each needs a defined recovery rather than a stall. Most of the final push from a working pipeline to a 95% success rate across arena trials came from enumerating those cases and giving each one a behavior.
