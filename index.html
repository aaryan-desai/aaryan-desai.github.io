About

Most of my work has happened on a factory floor, where a design is only as good as the cycle time it holds on a bad shift.

I came to automation through mechanical design — dies, fixtures, tooling — and kept going until I owned the controls and the data as well. That turns out to be the useful combination: the reason a robot cell misses its target is rarely in one discipline. A transfer that arrives with jitter is a motion-profile problem and a fixturing problem at once, and you cannot fix it from either side alone.

At Modineer I consolidated a robotic weld cell, re-profiled the die transfer that was gating it, and built the telemetry pipeline that made the constraint visible in the first place. At Tesla I spent two terms on production control equipment during a vehicle ramp, which is the fastest way I know to learn what modular actually means.

I'm now at Carnegie Mellon in the MRSD program, working on the autonomy side — perception, planning and control — with the intention of pointing it back at manufacturing.
