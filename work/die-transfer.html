Die Transfer Motion Profile

The transfer move gating the weld cell was taking 1.7 seconds and arriving with visible jitter. Re-profiling it — rather than commanding more speed — brought it to 1.1 seconds with the jitter gone.

The problem

The die transfer move was taking 1.7 seconds and arriving with visible jitter at the end of travel. Jitter at the end of a move costs you twice over: it adds settling time before the next operation can start, and it degrades placement repeatability, which shows up downstream as scrap.

So the move was gating the cell in two directions at once — on cycle time directly, and on quality indirectly. Either alone would have justified the work.

Worth stressing the third number: nothing was replaced. The 35% came out of the move's shape, not out of a faster machine, which is why it cost a tuning session rather than a capital request.

Approach

The obvious move — command a faster transfer — makes the problem worse. A higher velocity into the same deceleration produces more overshoot, more settling and more jitter, so the cycle time you win on travel you hand straight back on settling.

The gain came from tuning the acceleration and deceleration ramps and the blend through the path so the arm arrives already settled. Same endpoint, same hardware, a different shape — 1.1 seconds with the jitter eliminated.

Fill in

A real before/after trace, or the Power BI cycle-time chart showing the shift, is the single most convincing thing you could put on this page. A controls interviewer will read a trace faster than they will read this paragraph.

How it was found

Worth saying plainly: this move was identifiable as the constraint because the telemetry pipeline was measuring it. Before that, the cell was slow for reasons nobody could point at precisely. The 35% is a result of the instrumentation as much as of the tuning.
