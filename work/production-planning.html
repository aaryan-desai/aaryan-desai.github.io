Production Planning Tool

A UI-complete planning tool that allocates production through the E-Coat and Powder-Coat lines by FIFO, on a dependency graph of parts and their subparts — and pushes the planner's decisions to a live view on the floor.

RoleDesigned and built end to end
DisciplinesSoftware, data
LinesE-Coat, Powder-Coat
StatusIn use on the floor

The problem

Coating lines are a scheduling bottleneck of a particular kind: a part cannot go through until every subpart it depends on has been through, and the planner is the only person holding that dependency structure — usually in their head, or in a spreadsheet that goes stale the moment the floor deviates from it.

That makes the plan fragile in exactly the situation where it matters. When the line goes down or an order jumps the queue, re-deriving what can now run means walking the whole part tree again by hand.

Approach

A graph, not a list. Parts and subparts form a dependency graph, so allocation is a traversal problem rather than a sorting one. Modelling it explicitly is what makes "what can run right now" a query the tool answers instead of a question the planner re-derives.

FIFO as the default, not the verdict. First-in-first-out is the allocation rule, but the tool suggests and the planner disposes. That split matters: a scheduler that overrides the person who can see the floor gets switched off within a week, and one that hides its reasoning never gets trusted in the first place.

Two surfaces, one state. The planner works in a review UI; the floor gets a live view of what was actually committed. Keeping those as views of one state is what stops the plan and the floor drifting apart — which is the failure the whole tool exists to prevent.
