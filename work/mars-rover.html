The problem

An arena, a set of foam cubes, and a rover that had to find them, pick them up and place them — with no operator in the loop. Fully autonomous operation was the requirement, not a stretch goal, which meant every failure mode had to be handled on the rover rather than recovered by a human.

Approach

I led electronics, sensor integration and the autonomy software; the mechanical design and control system integration were collaborative, with the arm geometry and the software driving it developed against each other rather than in sequence.

  • Electronics. Power distribution, motor drive and the sensor wiring everything else depended on.
  • Sensor integration. Getting detection reliable enough that the autonomy above it could trust its inputs — where most of the debugging time actually went.
  • Autonomy. The detect, approach, grasp and place sequence, plus the recovery behaviours around it.

The last 25 points came from failure handling, not from the happy path. A cube detected at a bad angle, a grasp that slips, an approach that overshoots — each needs a defined recovery rather than a stall. Enumerating those cases and giving each one a behaviour is what moved a working pipeline to a 95% success rate.
